## 1. The "enum bug" is not a fleet-wide invisibility bug (§5 is wrong)

`src/ast/types.ts:206-216` — `ParseResult` carries imports on a **separate
channel** from relationships:

    relationships: UniversalRelationship[]
    imports: ImportInfo[]

and `parallel-indexer.ts:2169` persists imports with `INSERT OR REPLACE INTO
brain_import` — a **dedicated table** — never as `brain_relationship` rows.

So `RelationshipType.Imports = "brain_import"` names the *table*, not a
relationship-row type. Measured consequences:

| claim in §5 | measured |
|---|---|
| "16 analyzers emit the raw literal `imports`" | **6** emit an import *relationship* (GO, VB, HASKELL, C, ERLANG, JAVASCRIPT) |
| "`brain_import` emitted by zero of twenty lanes" | true — **0 of 20**, in every lane, and it is unreachable by design |
| "import edges invisible to every consumer" | **17 of 20** lanes fire imports into `imports[]` (JAVA 2089, TS 1144, VB 1060, CPP 916, GO 781 …) |
| "nothing reaches 10/10 before ENUM_HARD_FIX/ENUM_SWEEP" | the 16-emitter sweep would buy nothing; `imports[]` already works |

**The real defect is narrower and different in kind:** `Imports` is a dead enum
member (no emitter produces `brain_import` as a relationship type) while `"imports"`
is an off-enum literal that 6 emitters produce. That is enum hygiene plus a
naming collision with a table — a decision for the master, not 16 emitters to
sweep. `ENUM_SWEEP` as briefed would have had 16 agents editing working code to
chase a defect that lives in the denominator definition.

## 4. What this changes about the priority order

§8 says: land `ENUM_HARD_FIX`, then `ENUM_SWEEP`, and nothing reaches 10/10
before that. **Both are the wrong priority.** What the measurements support:

1. **`exports` is the real cross-cutting gap** — silent in **15 of 20** lanes.
   It is also the one silent type that legitimately belongs on the relationship
   channel, so it is real work, not a denominator artifact. The Java/Kotlin/
   Scala/Groovy/Swift/Ruby/ObjC/Clojure/CPP group should each answer: does the
   language have an export construct, and is it a `GAP` or a `RULED` with a
   corpus measurement?
2. **`VUE` 1/10 and `SVELTE` 3/10 produce zero imports.** Both delegate to the
   JS/TS analyzers and the delegation loses the import edges entirely. That is a
   real, confirmed defect and the highest-value single fix in the fleet.
3. **The `brain_import` denominator needs a decision, not a sweep.** Both 9/10
   lanes are silent on *only* `brain_import` — a type that cannot fire by
   construction. Either imports must also be published as relationships, or the
   denominator must count imports on their own channel. Rule 5 allows a
   denominator change behind a corpus measurement from a different source than
   the numerator; §1 is that measurement, but **the choice is the master's and
   should be recorded before anyone claims 10/10.**
4. **Three corpora must be re-acquired**: Python, C#, Perl. Until then those
   three lanes are **unmeasured**, and §4's numbers for them are not reproducible.
5. **Do not re-clone to USB.** §8.2 is obsolete; the corpus is already on disk.
